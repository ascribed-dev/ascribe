# Comment placement, anchoring, and lifecycle in document-editing and code-review tools (as of Oct 2026)

Method note for the report writer: findings marked **(fetched)** come from the page itself (read through a summarising fetch tool, so quoted strings should be treated as near-verbatim, not checked character by character). Findings marked **(snippet)** come from search-result excerpts only and the page was not opened; treat them as lower confidence. "Documented" = vendor documentation; "Opinion/complaint" = user or third-party commentary.

## 1. How do document tools (Google Docs, Word, Notion, Figma, GitBook, Confluence) place comments, link them to anchors, and handle clustering?

### Takeaway
Every document tool surveyed uses a highlight on the anchor plus a card in a right-hand margin or panel; none of the current ones relies on a drawn connector line, and Word explicitly removed its dotted connector lines in modern comments. All of them pair the "in context" placement with a second, full list view (panel/sidebar) that is filterable, and several offer a density toggle (full cards vs icons only).

### Cited Findings
- **Word (documented, fetched):** two views. Contextual view puts comments "in the Comments pane on the right margin near the insertion point in the text"; List view "opens the Comments pane on the far right and includes all active and resolved comments." — [Microsoft Support: Using modern comments in Word](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/word/using-modern-comments-in-word)
- **Word dropped connector lines (documented, fetched):** "we've made anchoring the comment to the content easier to understand and we no longer use the dotted lines you're familiar with from classic comments." Linking is instead by anchor highlight; selecting the highlight emphasises the related card, and "All Markup" shows anchor highlights on the canvas by default. — [Microsoft Support: Using modern comments in Word](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/word/using-modern-comments-in-word)
- **Word (opinion/complaint, snippet):** a technical-editing blog's test of modern comments reported "the functionality of using comments has been greatly reduced", affecting authors, editors, legal and education users, and that comments sit too far from the text for users without very wide monitors. — [CyberText: Word: Testing Modern Comments and issues found (May 2021)](https://cybertext.wordpress.com/2021/05/16/word-testing-modern-comments-and-issues-found/). Historical: this is 2021 feedback on the initial rollout.
- **Word classic balloons (complaint, snippet):** when the right margin fills with balloon comments, all balloons on that page get "squished" narrow and right-justified. — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5299588/classic-bubble-comments-become-narrow-right-justif?forum=msoffice-all)
- **Google Docs (documented, fetched):** three density modes: "Hide comments: Hides all comments and closes any open comments panel", a minimised mode showing icons that preview on hover, and "Expand comments: Shows full comments and replies". A separate "Show all comments" panel lists every comment, with tabs for all vs "for you" and a type dropdown for open vs resolved. — [Google Docs Editors Help: Use comments, action items, & emoji reactions](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/65129)
- **Google margin stacking (patent, fetched; a patent describes a design, not necessarily shipped behaviour):** Google LLC patent US10042832B1 (filed 16 Jan 2015, granted 7 Aug 2018) states the problem that with limited margin space annotations may be "positioned far from the portions of the document that they reference", and the remedy: "When the first position conflicts with the second position, the first annotation and the second annotation are combined to form a stacked annotation", expandable on click. — [Google Patents US10042832](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10042832)
- **Notion (documented, fetched):** inline comments have two display settings: Default shows "all page comments in the right side of the page"; Minimal shows "comment icons on the right side of the page. You'll have to click on a comment icon to open and see the comment". A comments sidebar lists all page comments and can "Filter comments based on person or status (whether the comments are open or resolved)". Page-level comments live at the top of the page ("Hover over the top of any page and click Add comment"). — [Notion Help: Comments, mentions & reactions](https://www.notion.com/help/comments-mentions-and-reminders)
- **Figma (documented, fetched):** comments are pins on the canvas ("Individual comments are represented by a [pin]. Hover over a pin to preview the comment."), plus a right sidebar in comment mode where you can "view, search, sort, and filter comments" (sort by date or unread). Clustering: "Multiple comments that are pinned closely to each other are consolidated into clusters" depending on zoom. — [Figma Help: View and manage comments](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041547593-View-and-manage-comments)
- **Figma (snippet):** pins attach to top-level frames, components or groups and move with the frame. — [Figma Help: View and manage comments](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041547593-View-and-manage-comments) (wording from a search excerpt that mixed this page with third-party guides; verify before quoting)
- **GitBook (documented, snippet):** comments attach to any block; a Comments side panel supports replies, mentions, reactions and filters (per page, open vs resolved, and by author). A May 2025 release added a popover so users can "add comments right alongside the content you're editing and check threads right in context", and commenting on deleted blocks or pages within a change request's diff view. — [GitBook changelog, 20 May 2025](https://gitbook.com/docs/changelog/may-2025/20-may-commenting-improvements-link-ui-improvements-a-new-dashboard-and-more); [GitBook changelog, 13 Nov 2023 side panel improvements](https://docs.gitbook.com/changelog/november-2023/13-november-side-panel-and-sidebar-improvements)
- **Confluence (documented, snippet):** inline comments are a yellow highlight on the text; resolving removes the comment and the highlight from the page; resolved comments are reachable by filtering for Resolved in the comments panel. — [Atlassian Support: Comment on pages, live docs, whiteboards, blog posts, and slides](https://support.atlassian.com/confluence-cloud/docs/comment-on-pages-and-blog-posts/)

### Inferences
- The dominant linking device is highlight + proximity + reciprocal emphasis on hover/select, not a drawn line. Word is the only tool found that had connector lines and it removed them, stating the new anchoring is "easier to understand". A connector line in the tool under review is therefore a departure from current convention; it is not shown to be wrong by any source here, but no surveyed product currently ships one.
- Two overflow strategies recur for clustered comments: stack/merge into one expandable item (Google patent, Figma clusters) or degrade the whole column to icons (Google minimised mode, Notion Minimal). Both keep the marker adjacent to the anchor and sacrifice the visible body.
- Every tool pairs the contextual placement with an exhaustive list. The list is where state that cannot be positioned (resolved, orphaned) ends up.

### Gaps
- No primary documentation found describing how Google Docs actually lays out overlapping margin cards today (the patent is the only source, and it is not proof of shipped behaviour).
- GitBook's current comments documentation page could not be fetched (the old URL redirects to a 404); GitBook claims rest on changelog excerpts.
- Confluence's exact margin/panel layout for inline comments was not verified from a fetched page.
- Did not find Microsoft's design rationale blog for modern comments beyond the support article.

## 2. How do code-review tools place threads (inline vs side panel) and what trade-offs are documented or criticised?

### Takeaway
Code-review tools overwhelmingly place threads inline in the diff, directly under the commented line, and compensate for the cost (threads pushing code down) with collapse/minimise defaults and a separate navigation list. GitHub's 2025-26 Files changed redesign moved toward minimised-by-default inline comments plus a discovery side panel.

### Cited Findings
- **GitHub (documented, fetched):** line and file comments are made in the Files changed tab; a Conversations menu on that tab lets you "discover and navigate to all the conversations in your pull request". Line ranges are selected by dragging or shift-clicking line numbers. — [GitHub Docs: Commenting on a pull request](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/collaborating-with-pull-requests/reviewing-changes-in-pull-requests/commenting-on-a-pull-request)
- **GitHub new Files changed experience (documented, fetched; public preview announced 26 Jun 2025):** "A new side panel helps you discover comments, including the ability to search for text." and "Code comments appear minimized by default, but you can change this behavior by pressing `i` or unchecking the Minimize comments preference on the settings menu." — [GitHub Changelog, 26 Jun 2025](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-06-26-improved-pull-request-files-changed-experience-now-in-public-preview/)
- **GitLab (documented, fetched):** review comments can be written "on the Changes tab or the Overview tab"; threads are resolvable from either. — [GitLab Docs: Merge request reviews](https://docs.gitlab.com/user/project/merge_requests/reviews/)
- **Gerrit (documented, fetched):** "Inline comments are displayed directly in the patch file under the code that is commented. Inline comments can be placed on lines or on code blocks", with the block "highlighted by a yellow background"; file-level comments are added via the File header. — [Gerrit: Review UI Overview](https://gerrit-review.googlesource.com/Documentation/user-review-ui.html)
- **Gerrit 3.4 (documented, fetched):** comment state (resolved, unresolved, draft) is summarised by chips below the commit message, clicking a chip filters the Comments tab, and the Comments tab shows "not just the comment thread, but also the snippet of code (where the comment was made) as context." — [Gerrit 3.4 release notes](https://www.gerritcodereview.com/3.4.html)
- **Reviewable (documented, fetched):** discussions start by clicking a diff line (line discussion) or the file name (file-wide); discussions not tied to a file live in a "Top level discussions" panel. Unreplied discussions increment a red counter and are grouped as To reply / Unresolved / Resolved. — [Reviewable docs: Discussions](https://docs.reviewable.io/discussions)
- **VS Code comments API (API reference mirrors, snippet):** a `CommentThread` is "a collection of comments representing a conversation at a particular range in a document"; `collapsibleState` controls "whether the thread should be collapsed or expanded when opening the document" and defaults to Collapsed; there is an optional `state` (`CommentThreadState`); a `CommentingRangeProvider` supplies the ranges that support commenting, and without one users cannot leave comments. — [vscode-api.js.org: CommentThreadCollapsibleState](https://vscode-api.js.org/enums/vscode.CommentThreadCollapsibleState.html); [vshaxe externs: CommentThread](https://vshaxe.github.io/vscode-extern/vscode/CommentThread.html)
- **Phabricator (complaint, snippet; historical, Phabricator upstream is no longer maintained and Phorge is the successor):** in long diffs with many inline comments, most of them done, hiding them one at a time is "annoying" and error-prone; the request was for bulk hide of all/done inlines, answered with a sticky bar offering several hide/show modes. — [Phabricator T8909](https://secure.phabricator.com/T8909)

### Inferences
- "Inline threads interrupt reading" is treated by vendors as a real cost: GitHub's redesign minimises code comments by default, VS Code's API defaults threads to collapsed, GitLab collapses resolved threads, Phabricator added bulk hide. A margin column sidesteps this cost for rendered documentation, which is where the tool under review differs from the code-review norm and aligns with the document-tool norm.
- Every code-review tool adds a second navigation surface (GitHub Conversations menu and side panel, Gerrit Comments tab with code snippet, Reviewable discussion counters, VS Code Comments panel). In-place rendering alone is not considered sufficient for finding threads.

### Gaps
- No vendor document was found that explicitly states the inline-vs-side-panel trade-off in its own words; the trade-off above is inferred from the mitigations shipped.
- The official VS Code API reference page was too large to fetch the comments section, and the extension-guide URL tried returned 404; API claims rest on mirrors.
- The VS Code GitHub Pull Requests extension's own behaviour (e.g. how it shows outdated or resolved threads) was not researched.
- Phorge-specific documentation was not consulted; Phabricator's archived docs were used.
- A search summary claimed GitHub shipped a dedicated Comments panel on the Files changed page on 19 Feb 2026 for general PR comments; I could not tie this to a primary URL, so it is unverified.

## 3. How does each tool handle comments whose anchor is gone or changed, and where are orphans surfaced?

### Takeaway
There are three strategies: (a) detach and label (GitHub "Outdated", GitLab outdated threads, Hypothesis orphans, Google Docs "Original content deleted", Figma unattached comments), (b) re-port to a best-guess location and mark it visually as approximate (Gerrit ported comments, Phabricator "ghost" inlines, Reviewable "nearest corresponding line"), and (c) silently resolve (Confluence). Vendors that re-port explicitly warn that the position can be wrong; tools that detach have drawn long-running complaints when the detached thread is no longer visible next to the new content.

### Cited Findings
- **GitHub rationale (staff statement, fetched, 29 Mar 2019):** "If the line that the comment was in relation to changed, the automatic tools have no way of knowing if the comment targeted at the old line still makes sense on the new line." Outdated comments were shown only in the Conversation tab with an "Outdated" flag. — [GitHub community discussion #23138](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/23138)
- **GitHub complaints (opinion, fetched):** users object that verifying a fix requires two tabs: "I can't do this in the Conversation tab view, since the fixed code isn't shown there" (Oct 2019); "This is one of the stupidest 'intended behaviors' ever. You need to have 2 tabs open" (May 2020). — [GitHub community discussion #23138](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/23138)
- **GitHub new experience (user report, fetched, Sep 2025):** "outdated comments are THERE! Granted they're at the top instead of inline, but I almost jumped for joy out of my chair"; later reports in the same thread say display of outdated comments is inconsistent. — [GitHub community discussion #23138](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/23138)
- **GitHub file-level comments (complaint, snippet):** file comments are marked outdated after any push, even when that file is untouched. — [GitHub community discussion #86527](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/86527)
- **GitLab (snippet):** when a push touches lines with diff threads, the threads are marked outdated and get a system note "changed this line in version N of the diff" linking to the comparison between MR versions, so reviewers can see how the concern was addressed. — [GitLab issue #24468: Display change that made thread outdated in-line](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-org/gitlab/-/issues/24468)
- **GitLab setting (documented, snippet):** projects can enable "Automatically resolve merge request diff threads when they become outdated"; "Threads on unchanged lines and top-level resolvable threads are not resolved." — [GitLab Docs: Merge requests](https://docs.gitlab.com/user/project/merge_requests/)
- **GitLab complaints (issue titles, snippet):** "Gitlab looses threads from diff view after pushing new changes"; a forum thread reports threads only track changes once. — [GitLab issue #276224](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-org/gitlab/-/issues/276224); [GitLab forum](https://forum.gitlab.com/t/merge-request-threads-only-track-changes-once/119903)
- **Gerrit ported comments (documented, fetched; introduced in 3.4):** "Unresolved comments that were left on older patchsets will now also be shown on newer patchsets." — [Gerrit 3.4 release notes](https://www.gerritcodereview.com/3.4.html)
- **Gerrit porting fallback (documented, snippet):** porting may drop the line/range and convert the comment to a file comment, or move it to a different file or to patchset level; "Callers of this endpoint shouldn't rely on the exact logic of which comments are ported as that logic might change in the future." — [Gerrit REST API: /changes/](https://gerrit-review.googlesource.com/Documentation/rest-api-changes.html)
- **Phabricator ghosts (documented, fetched):** "When a revision is updated, we attempt to port inline comments forward and display them on the new diff. Ported comments have a pale, ghostly appearance and include a button which allows you to jump back to the original context where the comment appeared." and "Ported comments sometimes appear in unexpected locations... In the general case, it is not possible to always port comments to the same lines humans would automatically." Users can turn it off via Settings, Diff Preferences, Show Older Inlines. — [Differential User Guide: Inline Comments](https://secure.phabricator.com/book/phabricator/article/differential_inlines/)
- **Reviewable (documented, fetched):** a line discussion "will also appear in the diffs of other revisions, at the nearest corresponding line" and "won't disappear until that discussion is resolved or withdrawn, even if the underlying file changes drastically." A red dog-ear indicates "the current context may be inaccurate because of significant changes that have been made since the time the comment was made in the original revision." — [Reviewable docs: Files](https://docs.reviewable.io/files)
- **Hypothesis orphans (documented, fetched, 1 Mar 2017, client 1.2.0):** "When annotations fail to anchor, we call them orphans". Previously orphans were hidden entirely, so users returning to a page thought their annotations were missing and direct links did not show them; the fix was a dedicated Orphans tab in the sidebar next to Annotations and Page Notes, shown only when orphans exist. — [Hypothesis blog: Showing Orphaned Annotations](https://web.hypothes.is/blog/showing-orphaned-annotations/)
- **Hypothesis orphan rate (research, snippet):** a study reported about 27% of highlighted text annotations could no longer be attached to their live web pages. — [Quantifying Orphaned Annotations in Hypothes.is (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283646490_Quantifying_Orphaned_Annotations_in_Hypothesis). Historical (c. 2015) and about the open web, not versioned repositories.
- **Google Docs (third-party issue reports, snippet):** when Docs cannot place an anchored comment, the comments panel shows it as "Original content deleted" with no text highlighted. — [googleworkspace/cli issue #169](https://github.com/googleworkspace/cli/issues/169); Drive API context in [Google Developers: Manage comments and replies](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/manage-comments)
- **Confluence (documented KB, snippet):** if commented text is cut and pasted elsewhere, Confluence treats the text as deleted and resolves the inline comment; it is preserved under Resolved comments but no longer visible in the page body. — [Atlassian KB: Keep inline comments while editing Confluence pages](https://support.atlassian.com/confluence/kb/keep-inline-comments-while-editing-confluence-pages/)
- **Figma (forum, snippet):** cutting and pasting a frame disconnects its comments; they remain viewable from the right sidebar. Users ask for reattachment and for a label such as "attached content deleted" so the cause is clear, saying it can be too hard to tell what an orphaned comment referred to. — [Figma forum: Make it possible to reattach unattached comments](https://forum.figma.com/suggest-a-feature-11/make-it-possible-to-reattach-unattached-comments-33090)

### Inferences
- Listing detached threads at the top of the page matches what GitHub's new Files changed experience reportedly does for outdated comments (user report) and is analogous to Hypothesis's Orphans tab. The Hypothesis history is the clearest evidence that hiding orphans is the worse failure: users assumed data loss.
- "Outdated" (anchor exists but content changed) and "detached" (anchor cannot be located) are distinct states in practice. Gerrit, Phabricator and Reviewable keep the first in place with a visual caveat (ghost styling, dog-ear) and a way back to the original context; GitHub historically collapsed both into "removed from the diff view" and was criticised for it.
- Three things users ask for when a thread loses its anchor: say why (Figma "attached content deleted"), show the original context (Phabricator jump-back button, Gerrit code snippet in Comments tab, GitLab version-comparison link), and keep it near the replacement content (GitHub complaints).
- Confluence's silent auto-resolve on cut/paste conflates anchor loss with resolution; GitLab makes the same behaviour an explicit opt-in project setting.

### Gaps
- GitHub's official docs page did not yield a definition of "outdated" in the fetch; the definition here comes from a staff reply in a community thread (2019) and user reports. Current inline behaviour of outdated threads in the new Files changed experience is not documented in sources I found.
- No official Google or Microsoft documentation found on what happens when anchored text is deleted in Docs or Word.
- No official Figma statement on comments whose frame is deleted (only forum reports about cut/paste and moves).
- Gerrit's user-facing docs page fetched did not describe ported comments' visual treatment.
- Notion and GitBook behaviour on deleted anchors not found (beyond GitBook allowing comments on deleted blocks in a change-request diff).

## 4. How are resolved threads treated, and what guidance or complaints exist?

### Takeaway
Document tools hide resolved comments from the contextual view and keep them in a filterable list; code-review tools collapse them in place. Both camps provide a route back and a reopen action.

### Cited Findings
- **Word (documented, fetched):** in Contextual view "resolved comments are hidden so you can focus on active comments"; List view includes active and resolved; a reopened comment "is visible again in the Contextual view." — [Microsoft Support: Using modern comments in Word](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/word/using-modern-comments-in-word)
- **Google Docs (documented, fetched):** Resolve closes the comment; the all-comments panel filters open vs resolved; resolved comments can be re-opened. — [Google Docs Editors Help](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/65129)
- **Notion (documented, fetched/snippet):** resolved comments are found by switching the comments sidebar filter to resolved, where they can be re-opened. — [Notion Help: Comments, mentions & reactions](https://www.notion.com/help/comments-mentions-and-reminders)
- **Figma (documented, fetched):** resolving "will hide the comment from both the right sidebar and the canvas"; a "Show resolved comments" filter brings them back. — [Figma Help: View and manage comments](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041547593-View-and-manage-comments)
- **GitBook (documented, snippet):** resolving hides the comment but keeps it in the Resolved tab of the comments side sheet. — [GitBook docs: Comments and discussions](https://docs.gitbook.com/getting-started/collaboration/comments-discussion) (URL now redirects; text from search excerpt)
- **Confluence (documented, snippet):** resolving removes the comment and its highlight; "filter for Resolved from the comments panel"; reopen available. An inline comment added and resolved within one editing session is visible in the resolved list only to people who can edit the page. — [Atlassian Support](https://support.atlassian.com/confluence-cloud/docs/comment-on-pages-and-blog-posts/)
- **GitHub (documented, fetched):** on Resolve conversation, "The entire conversation will collapse and be marked as resolved. This makes it easier to find conversations that still need to be addressed." — [GitHub Docs: Commenting on a pull request](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/collaborating-with-pull-requests/reviewing-changes-in-pull-requests/commenting-on-a-pull-request)
- **GitLab (documented, fetched):** "Resolved threads are collapsed, but users can still add comments."; expanding gives Reopen thread. — [GitLab Docs: Comments and threads](https://docs.gitlab.com/user/discussions/)
- **Gerrit (documented, fetched):** comments are "unresolved (something should be changed) or resolved (informational)"; quick replies "Done" and "Ack" resolve. Only unresolved comments are ported to newer patchsets. — [Gerrit: Review UI Overview](https://gerrit-review.googlesource.com/Documentation/user-review-ui.html); [Gerrit 3.4 release notes](https://www.gerritcodereview.com/3.4.html)
- **Reviewable (documented, fetched):** resolution is by participants' dispositions (Discussing, Blocking, Working, Satisfied, Informing), "independent of any file changes"; resolved line discussions shrink to a gutter marker: "There are one or more resolved discussions on this line. Click to reveal." — [Reviewable docs: Discussions](https://docs.reviewable.io/discussions); [Reviewable docs: Files](https://docs.reviewable.io/files)
- **Phabricator (complaint, snippet; historical):** done inlines cluttering long diffs led to a request for bulk hiding. — [Phabricator T8909](https://secure.phabricator.com/T8909)

### Inferences
- Collapsing resolved threads in place (the reviewed tool's choice) is the code-review convention; hiding them is the document-tool convention. Reviewable's single gutter marker per line is the most compact in-place form found and addresses the clutter complaint without removing the thread.
- Whichever is chosen, all tools expose a resolved filter in the list surface and a reopen action; lack of either would be an outlier.

### Gaps
- No vendor guidance found that argues for collapse-in-place vs hide; and no strong primary evidence of complaints about resolved comments "disappearing" in Docs/Word was collected (not searched in depth).

## 5. Narrow-width behaviour when there is no room for a margin column

### Takeaway
At narrow widths tools drop the margin and fall back to a marker on the anchor (highlight or icon) that opens the thread on tap, plus the full list panel. Documentation on this is thin; several vendors treat small screens as unfinished.

### Cited Findings
- **Google Docs mobile (documented, snippet):** on iPhone/iPad and Android, find the highlighted text, tap it, then choose "View comment". — [Google Docs Editors Help, iPhone & iPad](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/65129?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DiOS); [Android](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/65129?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)
- **Google Docs desktop (documented, fetched):** a minimised mode reduces comments to icons that preview on hover; this is a user choice rather than a documented automatic breakpoint. — [Google Docs Editors Help](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/65129)
- **Notion (documented, fetched):** Minimal mode shows only comment icons at the right that open the comment on click (user setting). — [Notion Help](https://www.notion.com/help/comments-mentions-and-reminders)
- **Word (third-party, snippet):** List view docks a resizable Comments pane; a commentator notes contextual comments are too far from the text without a wide monitor. — [Office 365 for IT Pros: How Modern Comments Work in Word for Windows](https://office365itpros.com/2021/05/21/how-modern-comments-work-word-windows/); [CyberText](https://cybertext.wordpress.com/2021/05/16/word-testing-modern-comments-and-issues-found/)
- **GitHub (documented, fetched):** the June 2025 Files changed preview announcement listed "Small screen (e.g. mobile device) support" among items still in development at that time. — [GitHub Changelog, 26 Jun 2025](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-06-26-improved-pull-request-files-changed-experience-now-in-public-preview/). May have changed since; not re-verified.
- **Reviewable (documented, fetched):** as the window narrows, "the diff panels will convert from a side-by-side view to a unified view and vice-versa", and code wraps to a column count proportional to window width. — [Reviewable docs: Files](https://docs.reviewable.io/files)
- **Figma (documented, fetched):** comments can be viewed and replied to from the canvas in browser, desktop and mobile app (pins need no margin). — [Figma Help](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041547593-View-and-manage-comments)

### Inferences
- "Marker that opens a panel" at narrow widths is the same pattern Google Docs mobile and Notion Minimal use, so the reviewed tool's narrow layout is conventional. The tools that expose it as a setting (Google, Notion) suggest some users want the compact mode even on wide screens.

### Gaps
- No documentation found for the exact width at which Google Docs or Word on the web collapse the margin automatically, or for GitHub mobile's thread presentation.
- Whether GitHub's new Files changed page has since gained small-screen support is unknown.

## 6. HCI/UX research and standards on margin vs inline annotation and robust anchors

### Takeaway
The standards and research position is consistent: store redundant selectors (position plus quoted text with context), resolve them from cheapest/most exact to fuzziest, and when re-anchoring fails show the annotation as an orphan rather than guess. Evidence on margin vs inline placement is sparse; one recent small study favours margin notes over inserting content into the document flow.

### Cited Findings
- **Hypothesis fuzzy anchoring (vendor blog, fetched, 22 Apr 2013; historical description, the implementation has since evolved):** three selectors are saved per annotation: RangeSelector (XPaths plus offsets), TextPositionSelector (character offsets in the whole document text), TextQuoteSelector (exact text plus 32-character prefix and suffix). Re-anchoring tries four strategies in order: from range selector, from position selector, context-first fuzzy matching, selector-only fuzzy matching, accepting a match "if the difference is within a given acceptance threshold". — [Hypothesis blog: Fuzzy Anchoring](https://web.hypothes.is/blog/fuzzy-anchoring/)
- **Hypothesis developer explanation (mailing list, snippet):** selectors sit on a spectrum from performant to reliable: the position selector is fast but brittle, the quote selector robust but requires slow fuzzy full-text search, and the position is used as a hint for where the quote search starts. — [Hypothesis dev list: recommended anchoring/selector](https://groups.google.com/a/list.hypothes.is/g/dev/c/JCH8BRkp-cs)
- **W3C Web Annotation Data Model (W3C Recommendation, 23 Feb 2017, snippet):** TextQuoteSelector describes a segment by quoting it plus passages before and after; selectors may be `refinedBy` other selectors, and multiple selectors given are alternatives for the same selection; because edits may change the selection it is "RECOMMENDED that a State be additionally used to help identify the correct representation". — [W3C: Web Annotation Data Model](https://www.w3.org/TR/annotation-model/)
- **Brush, Bargeron, Gupta, Cadiz (Microsoft Research TR MSR-TR-2000-95, Sept 2000; also CHI 2001):** "This paper reports the results of two studies examining user expectations for 'robust' annotation positioning in modified documents. We explore how users react to lost annotations, the relationship between types of document modifications and user expectations, and whether users pay attention to text surrounding their annotations." — [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/robust-annotation-positioning-in-digital-documents/); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/365024.365117)

### Inferences
- For a tool anchoring GitHub review threads (which carry path, line, side, commit, and a diff hunk) to rendered documentation blocks, the Hypothesis/W3C pattern translates to: keep the exact position, also keep quoted source text with surrounding context, try exact first then fuzzy, and classify the result as exact / re-anchored approximately / orphan. Phabricator, Gerrit and Reviewable all show the middle state needs its own visual treatment and a way back to original context.
- Split-attention theory cuts both ways for a margin column: proximity to the block is good, but a thread displaced far from its anchor by stacking recreates the split. This is the problem the Google stacking patent describes and is the argument for a connector line or reciprocal highlight when displacement occurs.

### Gaps
- No Nielsen Norman Group article on comment/annotation placement was found; none is cited.
- The Brush et al. specific findings (commonly summarised as: users weight unique words in the anchor, pay little attention to surrounding context, and prefer an orphan to a wrongly placed annotation) could not be verified from the page fetched, which gave only the abstract. Treat that summary as unverified until the paper itself is read.
- The arXiv margin-notes result is from a search excerpt; sample size and exact conditions not checked.
- No controlled study found comparing margin threads against inline threads specifically for code or documentation review.
