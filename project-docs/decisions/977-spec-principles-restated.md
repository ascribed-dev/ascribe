section: The language
---

**SPEC's design principles, conformance, and binding summary are restated to match the features they now cover, with no change in behavior.** Principle 3 is "no logic": authors write no conditionals, loops, variables, or operators, and anything a processor derives comes from something the content model declares (a registry, a source, an API) and is the same for every reader of a build, as `@operation`, a marked JSON example's schema, linked glossary terms, and `planned` are; principle 2 drops "never what it computes". §1.3 defines the three severities, advice among them. §1.4 says the rules for tables (a row's attribute block and availability, the plain output's column alignment) apply where a processor supports tables. §3.8's binding table, and §4's, name the two exceptions to "at the top of a section, a directive describes the section; anywhere else, it describes the block it touches": a container that binds (`@memo`) touches from its `@end`, and `@summary` with no primary describes its section from anywhere in it. §4.10's "nothing reads a memo" is "no processor resolves or publishes a memo", naming the two places its words are read (a generated file's memo and a memo file's `quote`), and says a memo is for a line or two in place, and a memo file for anything longer. _Why:_ the principles and the summary were written for the first vocabulary, and a reader of them would predict none of the newer features, which add no logic but derive content, bind by exceptions written only as paragraphs, or rest on a severity and an extension the introduction didn't name. _From:_ Kyle, [#977](https://github.com/ascribed-dev/ascribe/issues/977), 26 October 2026.
