---
section: The language
---

**In a line of a versioned build, a link to a page the line doesn't publish lands on the page that replaces it, or that it replaces, and else on the page in the nearest line that has it, as a link out; it's `link-page-dropped` only when no live line publishes the page.** A page rewritten at a new path says which page it takes the place of with `replaces`, a reserved frontmatter key naming a page by its file path, with no `#id`; a fragment can't have one, and two files never publish to one address. A link follows a chain of `replaces` to the page the line publishes (SPEC §5.2). "Nearest" is the closest newer line first, then the closest older. A link out keeps its text, and only the link is marked: the site output gives the `<a>` `data-ascribe-release-line`, the line as `[releases.<target>]` writes it, which the element library's stylesheet shows as " (3.5)" after the link; the plain output writes " (3.5)" after it; and the JSON output's page target carries `line`. A link keeps its `#id` when the page it lands on has a heading with that id, and otherwise drops it with advice, `link-fragment-dropped`. A link from the current line to a page only an older line has is advice, `link-into-older-line`. A `replaces` that names no page is `replaces-not-a-page`, and a chain that comes back to its page is `replaces-cycle`, in every build; a line that publishes a page and the page it replaces is `replaces-overlap`, and one that publishes two pages replacing the same page is `replaces-same-page`, each in the line where it happens. In any build that isn't versioned, the two pages are different pages. `ascribe link` says where a link goes in each line, under `lines`; `ascribe resolve` shows the plain output's " (3.5)" and nothing more. _Why:_ a page added or rewritten inside the window then costs no marks on the pages that link to it; a reader is sent forward rather than back when they can be; `data-ascribe-line` is already the parser's source-line attribute, and a link is the smallest thing to mark; a page rewritten under a new name often keeps a section's id, and losing the reader's place isn't worth failing a build for; a cycle is wrong whatever the build, while what a line publishes is that line's question. _From:_ the maintainer, on the [versions plan](https://github.com/ascribed-dev/ascribe/blob/395fe80d/project-docs/versions/README.md), decisions 12, 13, 39 to 42, and 44, 10 and 15 October 2026 (39 to 44 confirmed with [#507](https://github.com/ascribed-dev/ascribe/pull/507)); recorded when the plan closed, with [#502](https://github.com/ascribed-dev/ascribe/issues/502).
