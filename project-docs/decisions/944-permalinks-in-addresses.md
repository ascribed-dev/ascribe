section: The language
---

**What became of a permalink no heading or page declares is recorded in `ascribe.addresses`, under the key `permalink:` and its name: `[retired]` with a reason, `[moved]` to a page and heading, or `[moved]` to `permalink:` and another name for an alias (SPEC §7.6, §7.10).** `ascribe.toml`'s `[permalinks.retired]` and `[permalinks.aliases]` are still read, each table warned of (`model-permalinks-moved`), and `ascribe addresses init` moves them: a retired permalink with a `to` becomes a `[moved]` entry and keeps no reason, since `[moved]` holds a place and nothing else, and `ascribe.permalinks` records it as `Moved to` and the place. A one-time command rather than a fix of `ascribe check --fix`, because the move writes two files and makes one, which a diagnostic's fix, an edit to the file it's in, can't. `ascribe permalinks retire` and the editor's quick fix write `ascribe.addresses`, making it when there's none, and `retire` takes `--reason` or `--to`, not both. `permalink-removed` is `permalink-lost`, as `address-lost` and `anchor-lost` are. `ascribe.permalinks` keeps its format: it's the list a product checks against. A `permalink:` entry isn't compared with the base, as a page's address is; it's checked against the content, and its address is published from the permalinks list, not `_redirects`. _Why:_ two records kept the same promise, one in the file an author writes and one in the file Ascribe writes, with two retire commands; one file is one place to look, and a page move or id rename that `ascribe.addresses` records carries its permalinks' entries along. _From:_ Kyle, [#944](https://github.com/ascribed-dev/ascribe/issues/944), 26 October 2026.
