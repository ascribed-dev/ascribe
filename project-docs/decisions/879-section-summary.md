---
section: The language
---

**A section has a summary when its writer gives it one, with `@summary`, which takes `@note`'s two bindings (SPEC §4, "self (with primary), else following block"): `@summary` alone makes the next block the summary, which renders where it is, and `@summary: text` gives its own text, which the page's body doesn't show.** The following form is for a sentence the page already shows, so nothing is written twice; to pick a first sentence, the writer makes it a paragraph of its own. The line form is for when nothing on the page reads well alone, as for a lead-in or a diagnostic, as a page's `description` isn't in its body. Any section may have one, with or without a permalink; embedded help is its first reader, and the JSON output, the editor's hover on a link to the section, search results, and a site's link previews can read it later. It's optional, and nothing guesses one: from phase 3, `help.json`'s `summary` is the section's `@summary` or absent, and the first-paragraph rule of [decision #878](878-help-json.md) goes. The rules: one summary per section, and a second is an error, at the second; the line form goes at the top of its section, after the heading and its `@id`, as `@id` does, and the following form anywhere in the section, before the block it marks; the following form marks a paragraph only, since a summary is shown as a sentence or two and a lead-in takes the line form; the line form's text is inline content, as `@note`'s is (code spans, links, emphasis, and phrases); a summary in a fragment is the summary of the section it lands in, on each page that includes it, as a fragment's permalink section is cut from its recorded page ([the plan](../embedded-help/README.md)'s decision 7); and a summary inside a variant arm is an error until phase 6, which can give each arm its own. In the outputs, the line form writes nothing in the site and plain bodies; the following form's block is written as it would be without it, with no element, and review's source anchors are its own (a sentence in the [site render contract](../../docs/content/contracts/site-render.md)); and the JSON output carries the section's summary on its heading, its text and which form gave it. The diagnostics generator writes `@summary:` from each diagnostic's `when`, and each of the 85 with none gets a `when` in `diagnostics.toml`, so a `**When:**` label never reaches a summary. A section with a permalink and no summary is reported as advice by default, configurable in `[checks]`: summaries aren't tied to permalinks, and only this check ties them, for embedded help. Not chosen: a container, `@summary:` … `@end`, which holds a lead-in and its list but adds a form no reader needs once the line form exists; an attribute on the heading naming a paragraph by its place (`summary=2`), which breaks when a paragraph is added; and a `{render=true}` attribute, which makes one attribute change both where the text comes from and whether it shows, where the two bindings say each in their own spelling. _Why:_ phase 1's measurements of the docs' 326 `help.json` entries ([#885](https://github.com/ascribed-dev/ascribe/pull/885)) show no rule over the prose picks a good summary: the 240 diagnostics' first paragraph is their metadata line, 37 sections open with a paragraph over 300 characters whose first sentence would do, 7 open with a lead-in ending in ":", and 2 have no paragraph first; so a summary is something a writer, or an agent, decides. The check is advice so a project that only links to its permalinks isn't asked for summaries, and one that ships embedded help raises it to a warning. _From:_ the maintainer, 23 October 2026, on [#879](https://github.com/ascribed-dev/ascribe/issues/879), settling [phase 2 of the embedded help plan](../embedded-help/phase-2-marker-decision.md); phase 3, [#880](https://github.com/ascribed-dev/ascribe/issues/880), builds it.
