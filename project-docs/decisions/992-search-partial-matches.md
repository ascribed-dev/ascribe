section: The binary
---

**When no block has every word of a query, `ascribe search` lists the closest places as partial matches, and common English words don't count toward every word.** `ascribe search --format json` adds two fields to `schemas/search.schema.json` at `schema_version` 1, since neither removes a field or changes one's meaning: `stop_words`, the query's common words, as written, which a place needn't have, empty when every word is one; and `partial_places`, when nothing matched and a place needs two or more words or phrases, the places that have some of them, at most 10, those with the most first, then those with the most stop words, then in `places`' order, each a place as in `places`, its `match` covering the words it has, with how many it `has` and the words and phrases it `lacks`, as the query writes them. `places`, `total`, and `pages_matched` keep their meaning, counting only places that have every word a place needs, and the exit code is still `1` when there are none, so scripts don't change. The text says "No block has both words. These have 1 of 2:" in place of "The words aren't in any page.", and ends each place with what it lacks; `ascribe_search` returns the same. A stop word is a word outside quotes in a short English list (`how`, `do`, `I`, `the`, `to`, and the like), held in `ascribe_resolve::reading::query`; a phrase in quotes is always needed, and a query of only stop words needs them all, so it still finds something. The editor's search reads queries the same way. Words aren't matched by their forms (`sign`, `signs`, `signature`): that would widen every full match, changing what `places` holds for queries that work today, and it could only be English's, since a project has no language setting yet; a partial match already points at the block, and the docs say to search again with the subject's other words. _Why:_ an agent runs `ascribe search` before writing a page, in natural words, and was told "No place matches" for `webhook signature` beside a section called "Sign webhook events", and missed the page "Publish for AI agents" for `how do I publish llms.txt` because `how`, `do`, and `I` are rarely in one block with the rest; either way it wrote a duplicate page. _From:_ [#992](https://github.com/ascribed-dev/ascribe/issues/992), the agent-writer persona review's finding 1 and the agent-reader review's comment, with the product decision on the issue, 26 October 2026.
