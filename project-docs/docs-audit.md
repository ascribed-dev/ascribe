# Docs audit, October 2026

An audit of the user docs in `docs/content/` as they stand at Ascribe 0.4.0 on `main`, on 20 October 2026, against four questions: which pages are too verbose, which try to do too much, where a screenshot would do the work of prose, and where a page has drifted from the product. The last section says where Ascribe's own tools helped the audit and where they fell short.

It recommends and doesn't fix. Every claim of drift below was checked against the code, the spec, or `ascribe <cmd> --help` by whoever found it, and the ones marked † were checked a second time while writing this up. What couldn't be confirmed is listed as unverified, not as drift.

## Summary

The docs are accurate. Across 69 pages and about 120,000 words, the confirmed drift is 24 items, all small: stale "from the next release" sentences for features that shipped, lists that didn't grow when the code did, section numbers that moved, and one wrong address. Nothing a reader would follow into a wrong result, except [`open=true`](../docs/content/writing/directives.md#L115), an attribute no directive has.

The problems are shape, not fact:

- **Four pages are several documents each.** [The directive reference](../docs/content/writing/directives.md) (6,500 words) is a syntax explainer, the directive reference, a frontmatter reference, and copies of two guides. [Agents](../docs/content/agents/index.md) (6,000 words, 21 headings) is ten tasks under one how-to title. [Versions](../docs/content/versions/index.md) (3,600 words) reaches its task after 1,200 words of concept. [`ascribe init`, `sources`, `observe`, `permalinks`, `templates`, and `vale`](../docs/content/examples/sources.md) is six commands with nothing in common, filed under "Examples and drift", and 27 of its 32 inbound links come from other features.
- **Facts have second homes.** Option paragraphs re-narrate the generated option tables they sit under; JSON fields are written out as prose beside the generated schemas; a dozen rules are stated three to six times across pages. The `ascribe.toml` reference and its contract are two 26-section documents with the same titles, numbering, and tables.
- **The product is visual and the docs have one image.** The editor's twelve pages describe views, bars, lenses, badges, and menus in paragraphs of up to 240 words. About 80 places describe a screen; one has a picture.
- **The release left marks behind.** With 0.4.0 live, badges for 0.2.0 and 0.3.x head a third of the home page's list and about twenty entries of the directive reference, and three sentences still say "from the next release".

What the earlier [surface audit](surface-audit/findings.md#the-docs-site) found about the docs on 10 October (findings 37 to 44) is all done: every issue it raised is closed. The set has gone from 19 pages to 69 in the ten days since, which is where the shape problems come from.

## 1. Too verbose

Five patterns account for most of the length. Each is listed with its worst cases; the per-section notes have the rest.

### Options re-narrated after the generated table

[checks/check.md:27-92](../docs/content/checks/check.md#L27-L92) includes `_generated/cli-check-options.md` at line 14 and then explains `--stdin`, `--vale`, `--editor-build`, `--format`, `--summary`, `--code`, and `--fix` again, largely in the help's own words. [agents/commands.md](../docs/content/agents/commands.md) does the same in 100-to-200-word paragraphs ([:24](../docs/content/agents/commands.md#L24), [:49](../docs/content/agents/commands.md#L49), [:144](../docs/content/agents/commands.md#L144), [:153](../docs/content/agents/commands.md#L153)). A reference needs the table, the exit codes, the output shape, and what the help can't say.

### JSON fields written as prose beside the schema

The generated schemas in `contracts/json-reports/` are the home of every field, and these pages list the fields again in paragraphs: [check.md:119-156](../docs/content/checks/check.md#L119-L156) (two tables, 30 rows), [versions/release.md](../docs/content/versions/release.md) at lines 51, 68, 86, 104, and 130, [examples/sources.md](../docs/content/examples/sources.md) at lines 49, 106, 121, 135, 149, 172, 201, 267, and 291 (the `update` paragraph alone is about 230 words), and [command/questions.md](../docs/content/command/questions.md) at 26, 45, and 187. The same page shows `outline`, `link`, and `refs` as a `@snippet` of real output, which reads better and has one home. Either a snippet or the schema, not prose.

### Marks and badges the release left behind

Each `@available` mark renders as a badge line. With 0.4.0 live, [index.md](../docs/content/index.md#L16-L33) has seven marks for 0.2.0 to 0.3.1 on its 20 entries, [directives.md](../docs/content/writing/directives.md) about twenty, [getting-started.md](../docs/content/getting-started.md) four, and [templates/index.md:129-130](../docs/content/templates/index.md#L129-L130) documents a bullet "removed 0.4.0". [memo-files.md:64](../docs/content/writing/memo-files.md#L64) has a 0.4.0 mark inside a page whose frontmatter is already 0.4.0, and `ascribe render` shows it splitting the list in two. The 0.4.0 marks still inform; the rest is a convention for a release window that has closed, and nothing reports a direct mark that's older than the oldest live line (only `availability-left-behind`, for features).

### The `ascribe.toml` reference and its contract

[content-model/](../docs/content/content-model/index.md) (four pages) and [contracts/content-model](../docs/content/contracts/content-model.md) (five) have the same titles, the same §1 to §24 numbering, and the same key tables through shared fragments. Outside the fragments, 10% to 28% of each page's sentences appear verbatim in its twin (most in [output.md](../docs/content/content-model/output.md), whose routing paragraph is the contract's word for word); the fragments themselves carry contract wording (MUST) into the reference. What the contract adds is real but small: the per-rule tables in [validation.md §24](../docs/content/contracts/content-model/validation.md), the `ascribe.lock` format, `llms.txt`'s exact layout, and the link cache's file semantics. The reference's own §24 is three lines pointing elsewhere, and no page under `content-model/` links to the contract. The split as two parallel documents isn't earning its keep: the contract could keep only what's normative and its own (the rule tables, the file formats) and link to the reference for keys, and the decisions in §26 belong in `project-docs/decisions/`. Issue #285 shared the tables; the prose around them is where the duplication now lives.

## 6. The tools: where they helped, where they fell short

The audit ran `ascribe check`, `model`, `outline`, `refs`, `render`, `link`, `explain`, `fmt`, `diff`, `report`, and `permalinks list`, through the MCP server from this session and the binary from the six section readers. Here is what each did for an editorial audit, which is not what they were built for, and what an audit wanted that nothing answers.

### Useful

- **`check` as the baseline.** One run over 176 files: no broken link, anchor, snippet, or phrase anywhere. That ruled out a whole class of drift before reading began, and every "drift" below that line was semantic, which only reading finds.
- **`model` as the map.** Its `navigation` tree gave every page's title, type, and section in one call, and `types` gave the project's own definition of each page type, the yardstick for "trying to do too much".
- **`outline` for shape.** Headings with ids and line numbers in seconds; it made the one-H2 sidebar page, the 37-heading directive reference, and the five level-3 file formats in output-layout visible before reading, and it surfaced the memo on getting-started's "Create ascribe.toml" that explained a choice before anyone judged it.
- **`refs` for exposure.** Inbound counts said which pages a split must re-point (39 links into agents/index.md, 13 into one sidebar anchor), which feature really owns the six-command page (27 of 32 links from elsewhere), that no reference page links to its contract, and that the fragment term's definition is seen from 195 places.
- **`render` for the reader's view.** It showed what the source hides: the badge line each `@available` becomes, the 0.4.0 mark splitting a list in two, "GA 0.3.1, removed 0.4.0" as a bare line, the glossary linker inside a contract sentence, and the filled `{vscode}` phrase. Piped through `diff`, it measured the reference and contract pages line for line.
- **`link` for section numbers.** Asked what `assets.md#2-resolving-a-reference` is, it found the two wrong "§2" references in the archive contract. **`permalinks list`** found the wrong address in the directive reference. **`explain`** gave severities and fix text without opening the registry. **`report`** gave the inventory (pages by type, examples current, orphans) in one call.

### Fell short

- **`refs` doesn't take an image.** Asked about `images/review-site-preview.png`, it answered "doesn't exist", for a file that exists and is used; the same for the path from the working directory. Images are a target the surface lists and `refs` doesn't.
- **`refs` and `link` spell paths differently.** `refs` takes a path from the content root; `link` takes the target relative to the page's folder and the page from the working directory. Every reader hit "doesn't exist" once and re-rooted by hand. `refs` also reports `file:line:col`, not which anchor a link targets, so "which sections of this page are linked from outside" still needs grep.
- **`refs` finds links, not mentions.** The missing `permalinks list` and `templates status` were found by reading; nothing asks "where is this command named".
- **`diff` against history needs a loadable base.** Compared with a revision whose `ascribe.toml` had since-fixed errors, it stopped at "ascribe.toml at <sha> has errors"; fair, but it means the pages' history can't be read back further than the last model change.
- **`report links` and `report agents` need lychee and afdocs,** neither installed here, so the external links went unchecked. **`report pages`** was useful; **`model`** doesn't show `[listing]`, `[permalinks]`, `[consumer]`, or `[checks]`, so those keys were confirmed from the loader.
- **`render` is plain Markdown.** It can't show tabs, badges, or the lens as the site or the editor draws them, which is what the screenshot question needed; the live site and a browser did that.
- **Nothing measures.** No tool gives words per section, paragraphs over a length, images per page, `@available` marks older than the oldest live line (only `availability-left-behind`, for features), or a passage that appears on two pages. The verbosity and duplication findings came from a 30-line script and from reading. Nothing compares a page's prose with `--help`, a JSON schema, `package.json`, or surface.md, so every one of the 24 drift items came from a hand grep into the crates.

What an audit like this would use, if any of it is worth building: `refs` for an image and for a mention of a command; one path convention across `refs` and `link`; `outline` with a word count per heading; a `report` section for marks older than the live line and for pages with no image; and, the most valuable, a check that a reference page's option paragraphs name only options the command has, which is the one kind of drift a tool could hold.
